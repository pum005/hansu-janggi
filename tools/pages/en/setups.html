Your first choice before a game

Janggi Setup Guide

Before a Janggi game starts, each player chooses where their horses and elephants stand. This is called the setup (sangcharim). Each of the four arrangements changes which side is solid early on and which side can get out quickly.

How to read a setup

On the back rank there are two free points on each side between the chariot and the guard, four in all. Two horses (H) and two elephants (E) go there. On this site a setup is named from left to right as seen from your side.

For example, HEEH means, from the left: chariot · horse · elephant · guard · (empty point below the general) · guard · elephant · horse · chariot.

The four setups

SetupArrangementCharacter
HEEH (馬象象馬)Horses outside, elephants insideBoth wings look the same, so you can fight evenly on either side. The elephants sit near the palace and help guard the center.
EHHE (象馬馬象)Elephants outside, horses insideAlso symmetrical. The horses are near the palace and come out to the center quickly, while the elephants aim long at the flanks.
HEHE (馬象馬象)Horse-elephant on the left, horse-elephant on the rightThe two wings differ: on one side the horse is outside, on the other it is inside. It makes it easy to choose a side to attack based on your opponent's setup.
EHEH (象馬象馬)Elephant-horse on the left, elephant-horse on the rightThe mirror image of HEHE. Same character, opposite direction.

Horses and elephants play differently

To choose a setup, it helps to understand the two pieces first.

  • The horse has a short move but is blocked less often. It develops early, settles into good points easily, and stays useful in tight spaces.
  • The elephant jumps far and reaches across the board, but its long path is easy to block. It gets stronger in the middlegame as lines open up.

So putting the horses inside speeds up the fight in the center, while putting the elephants inside adds defensive cover around the palace. Neither is simply better; pick the arrangement that suits your style.

If you are just starting

  1. Start symmetrical: HEEH and EHHE look the same on both wings, so the moves are easier to remember.
  2. Stick with one: playing many games with the same setup teaches you where your pieces belong.
  3. Randomize the AI: set the AI setup to Random in the game settings to face many different arrangements.
