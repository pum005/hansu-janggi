The step after the rules

7 Janggi Strategies for Beginners

Once you know the rules, the biggest question is "What do I move first?" Following just these principles will help you last much longer against the Easy and Medium AI.

1. Open lines for your chariots first

The chariot is the strongest piece, worth 13 points, but it starts stuck in the corner. Move the soldier in front of it aside, or develop the horse and elephant first, so the chariot can use long files and ranks. The side whose chariots come out late usually loses the early fight.

2. A cannon needs a screen

A cannon can only move by jumping over one piece, so it gets stuck when the area around it is empty. In the same way, if you take away the screen of an enemy cannon, that cannon loses its power.

  • Always check whether your cannon has a piece in front of it to jump over.
  • If an enemy cannon aims at your general, you can block it by removing the screen or adding another piece in between.
  • Cannons can neither capture nor jump over each other, so a cannon makes an easy wall against the enemy cannon.

3. Guard the palace with your guards

The general cannot leave the palace, so it has few places to run when attacked. Keeping both guards beside the general covers the palace diagonals and front row. Don't push your guards forward carelessly in the opening.

4. Count trades in points

When pieces are traded, think about their values: chariot (13) · cannon (7) · horse (5) · elephant (3) · guard (3) · soldier (2).

Example: if you give up your horse (5) to capture the opponent's cannon (7), you gain 2 points. The game screen shows the current material score under the evaluation bar, so check it right after a trade.

Points are not everything, though. A cannon with no screen is worth less than 7, and a soldier that has reached the enemy general is far more dangerous than 2.

5. Push soldiers side by side

Soldiers can also move sideways, so soldiers standing next to each other protect one another. A lone soldier that runs ahead is easy prey. Advance them slowly, two or more together.

6. Three ways to answer a check

A check can feel alarming, but there are always only three answers: ① move the general ② block the line ③ capture the checking piece. Look first for a capture, then a block, and only then a general move. Moving the general first often walks it into the next check.

7. Learn the basic checkmate patterns

  • Two chariots on the lines: one chariot covers a row of the palace while the other checks on the next row, leaving the general nowhere to go.
  • Facing cannon: put a cannon on the same file as the enemy general with one screen in between. Unless the opponent removes or blocks the screen, it is check.
  • Horse and chariot together: the chariot drives the general into one corner while the horse covers the points inside the palace, taking away its escape squares.

These patterns become familiar only after you have fallen into them and built them yourself a few times. When you are stuck, press Hint to see the move the engine chooses.
